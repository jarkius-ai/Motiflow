# Product Principles

These principles guide product, design, architecture, and operational decisions.

## 1. Meaning over aesthetics

Every workflow begins with the intended business and audience outcome. Visual quality without strategic relevance is insufficient.

## 2. Reason before rendering

Research, narrative, symbolism, constraints, and direction are explicit stages. Rendering cannot silently replace creative reasoning.

## 3. One coherent direction

The system should converge toward a dominant narrative and metaphor before expanding into variations.

## 4. Structured artifacts over hidden chains

Each engine receives and produces validated, versioned artifacts. Important decisions must not exist only inside transient prompts.

## 5. Human control at consequential gates

Humans approve briefs, strategic direction, high-risk outputs, and final releases. Automation accelerates judgment; it does not erase accountability.

## 6. Explainability by default

Users should understand why a direction was proposed, what evidence informed it, and how confidence was derived.

## 7. Model-agnostic architecture

Providers and models are replaceable execution dependencies. Product behavior and data contracts remain stable.

## 8. Quality is measurable

Evaluation combines rubric-based critics, constraint checks, provenance, human review, and outcome feedback.

## 9. Composable workflows

Specialist engines can run sequentially or in parallel through a governed DAG. Every node has a clear contract, retry policy, and stopping condition.

## 10. Preserve creative memory

Decisions, rejected alternatives, feedback, assets, and learned preferences should improve future work without obscuring provenance.

## 11. Enterprise trust is a feature

Security, access control, auditability, data boundaries, and operational visibility are foundational requirements.

## 12. Build the smallest complete loop

Prefer a complete, testable workflow over a broad collection of partially connected capabilities.
